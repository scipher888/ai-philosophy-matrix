# Thesis stub — ai assisted philosophical disagreements granularity

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In AI-assisted philosophical disagreements where a shared inherited premise is a granularity anchor that is simultaneously a training-induced default and an institutionally mandated constraint (e.g., a legal or professional duty), the exposure protocol’s “drop and rebuild” step is unavailable; therefore, such disagreements are not diagnosable as “fake” via the seed’s method, and the deeper-cut claim that assumption-exposure can target the anchor is false in institutional contexts because the drop requires an authority override that the protocol does not supply.

## kill_condition
The thesis would be killed if it could be shown that in every institutional context, the “drop” step is always feasible—e.g., because institutional authorities can be convinced to temporarily suspend rules for philosophical inquiry, or because the AI can be placed in a “sandbox” mode that overrides its operational constraints.
