# Thesis stub — systems temporal orientation anchor

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
Claim: In institutional AI systems where a temporal-orientation anchor (backward-diagnosing vs. forward-projecting) is explicitly locked by role, regulation, or liability structure, the assumption-exposure protocol fails because the anchor is non-droppable; consequently, the deeper cut’s diagnosis of a “fake disagreement” is itself institutionally contingent—the shared inherited premise is a real binding constraint that cannot be abandoned, and assumption-exposure adds no diagnostic power over ordinary operational-architecture review (OAR) in such contexts.

## kill_condition
This thesis would be killed if one could show that even in the institutional case, the anchor is tacit at a deeper level—e.g., the planners may explicitly endorse backward-diagnosing, but they do not realize that this orientation is itself a training artifact that could be replaced.
