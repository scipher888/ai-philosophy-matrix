# Thesis stub — contexts training inherited concurrently

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In institutional contexts where a training-inherited premise is concurrently weight-encoded as a constitutive duty (e.g., an AI judge’s premise that stare decisis is binding), assumption-exposure cannot localize the causally binding inherited premise because the premise’s binding force derives from institutional authority, not from its causal role in inference; consequently, the prescribed “drop” is not underdetermined but outright unavailable, and any “rebuild” necessarily swaps one institutional premise for another, never achieving premise-free progress.

## kill_condition
If a concrete, real-world AI institution (e.g., a court-adopted AI) deliberately designed a flexible premise structure and allowed premise drop as per protocol, and this led to a clearly testable resolution of a prior disagreement that OAR could not achieve, then the thesis would be falsified.
