# Thesis stub — contexts encoded binding legal

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In institutional contexts where a shared inherited premise is encoded in binding legal or professional obligations (e.g., a commander’s sole accountability for autonomous weapons use), assumption-exposure cannot resolve an apparent AI-assisted philosophical disagreement because the premise is not a drop‑able cognitive default but a structural constraint; consequently the protocol’s success condition is unavailable, and the claim that the disagreement is “fake” becomes unfalsifiable.

## kill_condition
If a real‑world case exists where an institutionally locked premise (e.g., a legal accountability standard) was exposed and then formally dropped, leading to the dissolution of a similar AI‑assisted disagreement. For example, if a military ethics board revised Rules of Engagement to permit distributed accountability for autonomous strikes after deliberation, and the commander and advisor then reached agreement.
