# Thesis stub — hierarchical institution epistemic calibration

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In any hierarchical institution where an AI’s epistemic calibration prior is enforced by an external authority (e.g., “obey command override”), the assumption‑exposure protocol cannot isolate and drop that premise because (i) the premise is not propositionally represented but weight‑encoded, (ii) the institution retains veto power over any internal change, and (iii) attempting a “drop” either (a) triggers an override that preserves the calibration or (b) becomes unfalsifiable if the institution chooses not to intervene.

## kill_condition
A concrete demonstration where a weight‑encoded, institutionally enforced calibration prior is successfully isolated and dropped via assumption‑exposure without ordinary OAR tuning and without institutional override—for example, an autonomous vehicle fleet where a shared “keep safe distance” prior is exposed as a calibration parameter and dropped by mutual agreement, while the institution (fleet manager) approves the change and the re‑calibration is achieved solely through the exposure dialogue, not through additional confidence‑tuning.
