# Thesis stub — contexts constitutes mereological ground

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In institutional contexts where a shared inherited premise constitutes the mereological ground of the contrastive space for disagreement (e.g., the premise “duty to obey legitimate authority” in a military chain of command), assumption-exposure cannot coherently prescribe dropping that premise.

## kill_condition
This thesis would be killed if one could demonstrate a successful application of assumption-exposure in an institutional case where: - The shared inherited premise is the mereological ground of contrastive space, - The premise is weight-encoded and non-negotiable, - Yet the protocol yields a non-trivial, falsifiable result (e.g., after “dropping” the premise in a controlled simulation, the disagreement persists with a new intelligible structure, indicating it was real), - And this result is not achievable by ordinary OAR alone.
