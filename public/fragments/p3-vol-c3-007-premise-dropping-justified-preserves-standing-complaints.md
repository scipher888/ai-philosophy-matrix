# Thesis stub — premise dropping justified preserves standing complaints

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
Premise‑dropping is justified only if it preserves the standing of those whose complaints were intelligible under the old premise, by providing an equivalent basis for grievance in the new vocabulary.

## kill_condition
- **Counterexample**: Find a case where premise‑dropping destroys standing yet the overall outcome is morally better (e.g., the old complaint was itself based on a harmful premise, like racialised danger scores). If the standing‑preservation condition would block such a drop, the condition is too conservative – it locks in an unjust status quo. - **Empirical falsification** is not directly relevant (normative claim), but a demonstration that all premise‑drops *always* preserve standing would make the condition trivially satisfied and thus uninteresting. - **Alternative explanation**: showing that standing is not actually lost – that the old complaint was never really coherent – would undermine the condition’s force.
