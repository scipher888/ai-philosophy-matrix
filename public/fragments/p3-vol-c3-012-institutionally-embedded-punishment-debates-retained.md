# Thesis stub — institutionally embedded punishment debates retained

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In institutionally embedded punishment debates, retained hardship-forgiveness judgments can coexist with a genuine rejection of the suffering-desert premise because the premise functioned as a standing-conferring admissibility rule; a warranted successor must re-ground that standing without borrowing authority from the old premise.

## kill_condition
If a public, role-bearing, procedurally accountable mandate change supplies an equally accessible alternative standing mechanism, and human–model hardship rankings persist in otherwise identical paired cases, then the account is killed.
