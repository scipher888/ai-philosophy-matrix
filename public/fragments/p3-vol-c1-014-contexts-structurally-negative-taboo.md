# Thesis stub — contexts structurally negative taboo

Public fragment only — not a contest essay.

## claim
In institutional contexts where the shared inherited premise is a structurally negative taboo (a weight‑encoded prohibition on a class of moves, enforced by an authority that refuses removal), assumption‑exposure cannot distinguish fake from real disagreement because the premise has no propositional content to discard and the protocol’s “drop” step is unavailable; the subsequent “rebuild” reduces to standard OAR, confirming that assumption‑exposure adds no diagnostic power over ordinary OAR and rendering the original “fake disagreement” claim unfalsifiable in such cases.

## kill_condition
The thesis would be killed if an empirical demonstration showed that assumption‑exposure can successfully expose and drop such weight‑encoded taboos – e.g., by using a fine‑tuned model that can temporarily suspend the filter for analysis, or by having the philosophers use a separate “expository” AI that can articulate the taboo as a proposition without triggering it.
